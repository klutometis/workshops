Culture as represented in EU4 is the representation of the local populaces on a provincial level. The culture represents the combination of the local language, customs, ethnicity and nationality of the people. Religion is split from this list, as it is represented by provincial religion which has a separate representation in the game.

Culture groups in 1444
Individual cultures in 1444

The cultures of EU4 are divided in culture groups consisting of multiple cultures which have similar customs, have a similar language or have a shared history. EU4 has a total of 72 culture groups with multiple different cultures as represented with the two maps to the right.

Primary cultureEdit

 
Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.29.

All countries have one primary culture. This is normally fixed, although it can be changed either through the culture mechanics or via certain national decisions. The main interaction of the nation with local culture is by spending diplomacy points  .

Cultural shiftEdit

Any accepted culture that has at least 50% of the development of the country’s state cores can be promoted to the primary culture of the nation. This costs 100   diplomatic power, and replaces the previous ‘Accept Cultural Shift’[1] decision. The previous primary culture will consequently become an accepted culture.

The country cannot shift to a culture that is accepted due to the cultural union granted by Empire level government. Shifting the culture to outside the players cultural union shifts the cultural union, enabling the player to shift back to the preferred culture in the original cultural union.

It can be advantageous for a player to set a new primary culture to be able to form a country with a different tag (with new national ideas), or to be in a larger culture group, to have more brother cultures and save promoted culture slots.

If the nation has very few states active, it can state and de-state provinces to frequently change the primary culture to obtain Nationalism casus belli on more countries, as well as to reduce the time of coring.

Culture group shiftEdit

Originally only available via Sinicization, the game has included several ways to switch the groups a certain culture belongs to. Mechanically this is achieved by converting all provinces of the original culture to a new (usually similarly named) culture, which is part of a different group. The following is a list of all cultures that can shift what group they belong to and how:

  • Korean to Sino- Korean, switches from Korean to Chinese group. Via the "Sinicize our Culture" decision.
  • Oirat, Khalkha, Mongol and Korchin cultures to Sino-Altaic, switches from Altaic to Chinese group. Via forming   Yuan (can be formed by any Altaic culture nation but shifts only the four mentioned cultures).
  • All Tibetan group cultures to Sino-Tibetan, switches from Tibetan to Chinese group. Via the "Sinicize our Culture" decision.
  • Vietnamese to Sino- Vietnamese, switches from Mon-Khmer to Chinese group. Via the "Sinicize our Culture" decision.
  • Zhuang to Sino- Zhuang, switches from Tai to Chinese group. Via the "Sinicize our Culture" decision.
  • Jurchen to Manchu, switches from Evenki to Chinese group. Via forming   Manchu.
  • Georgian to Greco-Georgian, switches from Caucasian to Greek group. Via   Georgias "Throne of the Romans" or   Trebizonds "Georgian Union" mission
  • All British group cultures to Anglois, switches from British to French group. Via the   Angevin Kingdoms "The Angevin Culture" mission (Shifts only all owned and subjects owned provinces).
  • Bosnian, Bulgarian, Byelorussian, Croatian, Czech, Muscovite, Novgorodian, Polish, Ruthenian, Ryazanian, Serbian, Silesian, Slovak, Slovene and Sorbian switch from West Slavic, East Slavic and South Slavic to Slavic group. Via the   Russian "Protect the South Slavs" mission (All cultures keep their names in game and are only renamed internally).
  • Turkish, Azerbaijani and Turkmeni to Turco-Iranian, switches from Levantine, Iranian and Altaic to Iranian group. Via the   Aq Qoyunlu /   Qara Qoyunlu "Shahanshas of Eranshahr" mission (Shifts only all owned provinces of the three mentioned cultures).
  • Gothic switches from Byzantine to Germanic group. Potentially via   Theodoros "A Merchant's Family Home" mission. (Culture keeps its name in game and is only renamed internally).

Unique MechanicsEdit

Many cultures have governments, events, missions, decisions, and naval doctrines associated with them. In addition, some cultures also have unique mechanics at their disposal. These are:

  • Holy orders, which are available to Iberian   Catholic and Andalusian   Muslim nations
  •   Banners, which are available to Jurchen, Manchu, and Sino-Altaic nations.
  •   Samurai, which are available to Japanese nations.
  •   The Marathas Estate, which is available to   Indian Sinhalese Nations and   Indian Nations in the Dravidian or Western Aryan culture group (except Malvi or Rajasthani)
  •   The Rajputs Estate, which is available to   Indian Malvi and Rajasthani Nations as well as   Indian Nations in the Hindustani, Central Indian or Eastern Aryan culture group (except Sinhalese)

If the number of possible promoted cultures is reduced (e.g.:removing a policy) and there are now more accepted cultures than possible accepted cultures, 1 culture that was most recently accepted will be demoted at the end of the month. Unlike manual demotion, this will neither cost diplomatic points nor create unrest in affected provinces.

Cultural unionEdit

Cultural unions will automatically accept all sub-cultures within their respective culture group. Any nation can become a cultural union of its culture group upon reaching the   Empire government rank, or   1000 development. As part of this change, it is possible for several nations to be a cultural union for the same culture group. A nation can become an empire as part of a mission, decision, event, reaching a certain development level or by upgrading the government rank; cultural unions make it so that sister cultures don't have to be promoted to be accepted by the country.

Penalties for unaccepted culturesEdit

A province with a non-accepted culture will provide less tax and have higher unrest alongside other penalties. A province with a culture in the same culture group, but not the same culture as the owner will get lighter penalties:

Same culture group (Culture name will be in yellow, and will have a star next to their names on the culture tab)

  −15% Local tax modifier
  −15% Local manpower modifier
  −10% Local sailors modifier

Different culture group (Culture name will be in red)

  −33% Local tax modifier
  −2% Local missionary strength
  −33% Local manpower modifier
  −20% Local sailors modifier
  +2% Local unrest

Republican Cultural Sufferance

Republics get an additional cultural sufferance modifier in different culture-group provinces. This stacks with the usual penalty, mitigating but not eliminating it.

  +10% Local tax modifier
  +10% Local manpower modifier
  +5% Local sailors modifier
  −0.5 Local unrest

Culture conversionEdit

The culture of a province can be changed to another culture if the province is cored by its owner, has the same religion as its owner (for   Confucian, harmonized religions work as well), and has no unrest from separatism. The cost for converting culture is   10 diplomatic power per development[2] and the duration for the change to take place is 10 months per development.[3] Like coring costs, culture conversion costs are capped at 30 development, so improving a 30 development province will not increase the cost of converting its culture. Sieges and occupations will pause culture conversion but will not cancel it; the culture conversion will continue as soon as the province is liberated, although conversion will be cancelled if the province's ownership changes. If the conversion is cancelled before progress reaches 10%, all diplomatic points will be refunded.

A province can have its culture changed to the primary culture of the province's owner. With   The Cossacks, a province's culture can also be changed to any owned neighbouring province's culture or its culture can be restored to whatever it was originally.

Converting a province from culture A to culture B will incur a relations hit with every country whose primary culture is culture A.

Primary nationEdit

Most cultures have a primary nation. These nation will keep uncontested cores indefinitely[8], so long as their cores are in provinces of their culture - culture conversion will force these cores to obey the normal 50/150 year expiry timers for cores depending on whether or not the new culture is the same culture group as the nation with a core there. Additionally the primary nation is the default separatists target of a province if no other valid target exist even with no core on it. Primary nations of each culture can be found in the list below.

Lost culturesEdit

 
Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.33.

Cultures in the Lost Cultures group do not exist at game start, but can be created as custom nations or during the invasion. The only Lost Culture that can be re-discovered is the Roman culture by Restoring the Roman Empire that changes the primary culture of the nation. All provinces with a culture in the culture group of the former nation's primary culture will change to Roman culture. However you can change to any Lost Culture using console commands, by typing in the console "change_culture_court ruler [Culture]" then typing "event culture_religion_events.22" this will fire an event with one of the options being changing the nation's primary culture to the ruler's culture.

Other cultures that do not exist at game start can sometimes be created by event, such as the Return of the Norse culture, emergent colonial cultures in Colonial Nations for American, Mexican, and Brazilian culture, and Anglois for those who restore the Angevin Kingdom. However, these are not in the Lost Cultures culture group (though Norse was formerly in it)

Culture groupsEdit

Note that culture groups are not just based on linguistic criteria - for example, Romanian is grouped with the Carpathian cultures due to cultural similarities and for gameplay purposes, even though Carpathian is not a linguistic family.

Primary nations of each culture are listed to the right of each culture.

With   Domination there exists a additional culture group - Slavic. It can be founded via the Russian missions and combines all cultures of the East Slavic, West Slavic and South Slavic groups, except albanian, into one group.
