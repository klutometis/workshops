      A native tribe is the government form of all native tribes in North and South America that are not Nahuatl, Mayan or Inti faith, and all Aboriginal tribes in Australia.

      These governments don't use legitimacy, republican tradition or any other type of government strength value. There is no heir system: at ruler's death, a new one is generated with random stats.

      If a Native Tribe is forced to become Nahuatl, Mayan or Inti, they will lose the Native Tribe government and instead turn into the type of government of the nation that converted them. As these religions remove all access to government reforms, the nation will then have to reform via religious reforms.

      Game interface[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.31.
      The native interface showing a Federation led by Coweta.

      The Natives interface is a small shield (with a picture of a totem-like face) on the bottom of the screen (only visible to North and South American and Australian tribes as long as they have not reformed their governments, and the Conquest of Paradise DLC is active).

      Within the interface are three main sections: the left half shows the various Federation advancements available. The right-hand side shows the federation the country is currently in, if any, on the top, with the shields of all participating countries at the bottom. The bottom-right corner shows the tribal development of the nation indicating the development that is added to the capital on top of the development of the province.

      Tribal land[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.32.

      Natives often had traditional migratory land that they claimed but did not permanently inhabit. In-game, this is represented by tribal land. Tribal land provinces are not fully settled and do not directly provide income or manpower to their owners. On the political map, they appear uncolored (like uncolonized land) but with the name of the tribe superimposed. Colonial powers may ignore the tribal land claim and settle it directly without needing to declare war on the tribe.

      Migratory tribes may add land that they currently occupy (so long as its not the tribal land of any other nation) as new tribal land at the cost of 100 administrative power + 10 administrative power for every existing tribal land province. Additionally, all conquered provinces will be turned into and added to the nations tribal land.

      Settled tribes may add unclaimed lands bordering existing provinces for the same cost and won't automatically convert conquered provinces into tribal land.

      Tribal lands may be taken in war like any other provinces (but at a lower cost). If a non-native conquers tribal land, it automatically settles the land, otherwise its added to the conquerors tribal land.

      Integrating another tribe as a native tribe will add all their native land to yours.

      Despite not directly conferring resources like settled land, tribal land still has several benefits:

      • Tribes get a casus belli on any nation that borders or inhabits their tribal land.
      • Tribes may only migrate to provinces in another tribe's tribal land once every 2 years.
      • Settled tribes may use tribal development to permanently settle tribal land, turning it into a regular province.
      • A tribe that reforms out of primitive status will immediately settle all tribal land

      If a tribe loses tribal government but remains primitive (e.g., as a result of converting to a Mesoamerican religion), all tribal land is lost.

      Tribal development[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.32.

      Tribal development is a special form of development that native tribes possess in addition to standard province development. Tribal development is added to the existing development of the capital province, increasing the tax, goods produced, and manpower of that province just like regular development. Hovering the mouse over the development listed in a tribal province window will list the breakdown of province and tribal development contributing to the total. However, tribal development is tied to the tribe, not the province, and moves to the new capital if the native tribe migrates.

      Every month, one of the three development categories is increased by the total tribal development growth the nation has. Which category will be increased is selected randomly but hovering over the tribal development interface will show which category will be increased next.

      Natives with the Settle Down reform may settle all of their tribal development into a province (tribal land or fully owned), permanently adding it to the base development of that province. This costs 50 administrative, diplomatic and military power, regardless of the amount of tribal development. Doing this to a tribal land province turns it into a fully owned province.

      Tribal development can be increased by the following :

      • Grazing (only for migratory tribes) while inhabitating a province with less than 100% devastation:
        • +0.02 tribal development growth while inhabiting their own land
        • +0.1 tribal development growth while inhabiting another tribe's tribal land instead
      • The "Irrigation" native building provides +0.01 tribal development (migratory tribes may only have one Irrigation, since they only control one province)
      • The Joint Grain Depots Federation advancement provides +0.02 tribal development
      • The Indigenous idea 1: Bountiful Land provides +0.015 tribal development

      If total Tribal development is above 1/1/1, Tribal development growth is reduced by the following amount:


      ( total tribal development growth total tribal development ) {\displaystyle ({\frac {\text{total tribal development growth}}{\text{total tribal development}}})}


      This results in the following formula for actual tribal dev growth (while above 1/1/1 tribal dev) :


      actual tribal dev growth = total tribal dev growth − ( total tribal development growth total tribal development ) {\displaystyle {\text{actual tribal dev growth}}={\text{total tribal dev growth}}-({\frac {\text{total tribal development growth}}{\text{total tribal development}}})}


      Additionally, a tribe over its governing capacity cap will not gain any tribal development.

      To optimize tribal development, a settled tribe should always settle their tribal dev into their provinces once the tribal dev reaches 1/1/1.

      All tribal development is lost if a native tribe is conquered, or if they reform (because of changing goverment type). Thus a tribe should always settle their tribal dev before reforming.

      Grazing[edit | edit source]

      Native Tribes that have not enacted the Settle Down reform will slowly raise the devastation of the provinces they inhabit. To counteract this, Tribes are supposed to Migrate regularly when devastation gets too high.

      Grazing will cause monthly devastation equal to 6,66% of total Tribal development (one tenth of two thirds). Since every tribe starts with 1/1/1 (3) tribal dev, every migratory tribe will start with a monthly gain of +0.2 devastation.

      Provinces with 100% devastation wont grant their tribes any tribal dev growth, thus migratory tribes will be forced to migrate more often the longer the game progresses, if they want to keep their Tribal development growing.

      Migration[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.31.
      The migration interface, showing Fox's migration options at the 1444 start date, the selected province is part of Potawatomi's tribal land.

      A single-province native nation may migrate to an unoccupied non-wasteland province directly adjacent as long as it did not enact the reform Settle Down. Doing so costs 50 military power and has the following effects:

      • The nation moves to the selected province, occupying that province and leaving their previous province unoccupied.
      • The selected province will gain base tax and manpower from its native population as if it had been colonized and turned into a city.
        • The previous province retains its original native population.
      • Any buildings in the original province are moved to the new province.
      • Any of the nation's units in the old province are instantly transported to the new province.
      • The nations tribal development is removed from the previous province and added to the new one
      • If the province was not part of tribal territory it can now be added for a base cost of 100 administrative power plus 10 for each existing tribal province.
      • If the province was part of another nation's tribal land the owner of the tribal land gets the "Force Migration" casus belli against the invading tribe.

      Some ideas and effects reduce migration cost:

      Traditions Ideas Bonuses Policies
      −20%
      • Charruan traditions
      • Tapuian traditions
      • Plains Native idea 1: Dwellers of Tipis
      		 — —

      Each point of negative stability gives a −10% modifier to migration cost, up to a −30% reduction at −3 stability.

      Migration cost modifier is multiplicative with stability. Migrating with -2 stability costs 50 * 0.7 * 0.75 = 30

      Migrating within the nations own tribal land, or land that isnt the tribal land of any other nation has no cooldown, however migrating into another nations tribal land will give the nation a cooldown of 2 years before they can again migrate into another nations tribal land (unowned/selfowned provinces can still be migrated to).

      Gaining a additional province will disable a nation's ability to migrate, but it can be regained if a country is reduced back to a single province (this only concerns Sibirian Tribes and Polynesian Tribes).

      Migration cannot be done while at war.

      Federation[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.31.

      A Federation is a loosely defensive alliance available only to native nations. Federations are a useful tool for smaller tribes to combat aggressive expansionists, somewhat similarly to coalitions; should a member be attacked, all members within the federation will join on the side of the member. They do not take up a diplomatic relations slot. Eventually by taking Federation Advancements a federation can establish a federal constitution becoming a single nation under the control of the federal leader.

      A federation can be created by using the “Invite to Federation” diplomatic option on a willing country (only countries with the Tribe government type are eligible to be invited). The country that creates a federation becomes its first leader. A federation has a Federation Cohesion score that can be between 0 and 100 and increases or decreases each month. Whenever the federation cohesion reaches 0 the strongest nation in the Federation is chosen as the new leader and the Federation Cohesion is reset to 50.

      Each federation member has a "Strength" value that is calculated as follows :


      Federation member strength = ( Maximum manpower 1000 ) {\displaystyle {\text{Federation member strength}}=({\frac {\text{Maximum manpower}}{1000}})}


      The federation leader also receives:[1]

      +1% Diplomatic reputation
      +10% Morale of armies

      Federation Cohesion[edit | edit source]

      Cohesion In/Decreases every month by :

      • +0.1 per Member that has lower Strength than the leader and that is in the same culture group as the leader
      • +0.05 per Member that has lower Strength than the leader and that is in another culture group as the leader
      • -0.2 per Member that has higher Strength than the leader and that is in the same culture group as the leader
      • -0.4 per Member that has higher Strength than the leader and that is in another culture group as the leader
      • +0.5 per non- primitive nation bordering a federation member, per member
      • -0.05 per enacted federal advancement

      All Federal Cohesion changes are doubled (bonus of 100%) if at least three nations that are in the same culture group as the Federation leader are part of the Federation. This is applied to both positive and negative effects.

      Joining and leaving[edit | edit source]

      Countries will be more likely to join a federation based on their opinions of all federation members, how threatened they feel, the sum of the diplomatic reputation of the Federation, the primary culture group of the leader and the invited nation (-100 if it is different), the total size of the Federation army, the average distance to the Federation members, and the desire to remain in the current federation if they are already part of one. Countries who are part of a federation may leave at any time, and the leader of a federation gains a “Kick from Federation” diplomatic option to use with federation members. If the leader's opinion of a federation member drops below zero an AI federation leader will automatically kick the offending member from the federation. Likewise if an AI member's opinion of the leader drops below 0 it will immediately abandon the Federation.

      Note: Federations are separate from alliances; it is possible for nations in a federation to also be allies, but is it also possible that a nation may have no allies in the federation it is in, and multiple allies outside it. Likewise, it is also possible for members of a federation to be members of different alliances. If a Federation member declares war to another member of the same Federation the other members will not be automatically dragged into the war.

      Native buildings[edit | edit source]

      Native nations have access to a unique set of buildings to represent the advancement of a tribe as it grows bigger over time. Native buildings behave like normal buildings, being built in a building slot in a province and will bestow their effects upon that province. Two types of native buildings exist: Normal and Unique. Normal native buildings can be built once per province and will usually apply their effects only to the province they are located in. Unique native buildings can only be built once per nation but will apply their effects to the entire nation.

      All native buildings will be removed if the province they are in is acquired by a non-tribal nation, whether through enemy conquest or through the owner reforming their government. Migrating to a new province however will not remove all existing native buildings it will instead bring the buildings in the new province. Settled tribes that become migratory will keep the buildings in the capital, the others will be lost as their other provinces turn into tribal land. Reforming the government will remove all native buildings as well as the ability to build them. In exchange for that, however, the nation will gain the ability to construct and use normal Buildings.

      All native buildings have a base construction time of 12 months. All native buildings are lost after reforming the government. However, 35 days after changing the government, certain ones will be replaced by a regular building as shown below[2].

      Casus belli[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.31.

      Native nations are not allowed to fabricate claims, but can gain claims through events or missions. (They can also declare war without a casus belli and conquer provinces that way). They have access to some unique Casus Belli :

      Humiliate[edit | edit source]

      Available only with the Conquest of Paradise DLC enabled.

      A native tribe with only a single province gains a special casus belli called “Humiliate” against any adjacent single-province native nation (this is distinct from the “Humiliate Rival” casus belli, and can be used against any nation, not just rivals). The “Humiliate” casus belli enables a special peace deal named “Show strength” at 100% war score which causes the target to lose 20prestige and the victor to gain 100 monarch power in each category. This casus belli cannot be used to annex the target nation nor vassalize them.

      Tribal border feud[edit | edit source]

      A native tribe gains the "Tribal Border Feud" CB against any other native tribe whose tribal land borders theirs. This CB allows the annexation of provinces and tribal land but not the subjugation of the defender.

      Force Migration[edit | edit source]

      A native tribe that is migratory gains the "Force Migration" CB on any other migratory native tribe that migrates into their tribal Land. This CB allows the displacement of the trespassing tribe, aswell as the annexation of their tribal land. It does not allow the subjugation of the defender.

      Push back the colonizers[edit | edit source]

      A native tribe whose tribal land borders any non- native tribe, gains the Push back the colonizers CB against them. This CB allows the annexation of provinces but not the subjugation of the defender. It also allows the peace term "Abandon Colonies" which removes all colonies within the native tribes tribal land.

      Reforms[edit | edit source]

      Native Tribes have −100% Reform progress growth so most if not all of their reform progress will come from Monthly reform progress. Base Monthly reform progress for native tribes is +0.5, the unique Ceremonial Fire Pit building provides +0.2 Monthly reform progress, and each Longhouse provides +0.1 Monthly reform progress. The Oral Tradition government reform and Indigenous idea 3: The Great Law each provide a +25% Monthly reform progress modifier. This modifier also applies to regular reform progress if the tribe gains a bonus to Reform progress growth or reforms out of the Native Tribe government (reforming will lose the Oral Traditions government reform however).

      Reforming the government[edit | edit source]

      Please help with verifying or updating this section. It was last verified for version 1.31.

      New World nations with the "Native Tribe" government (not to be confused with similarly named governments found in the Old World) have specific mechanics that allow them to reform their government. This requires different conditions and provides different effects depending on if Conquest of Paradise is enabled. Reforming the government is the only way to become a normal nation state and get rid of the "Primitive" status.

      There are three distinct ways to reform the government:

      Reforming from foreigners[edit | edit source]

      Conditions :

      • If the DLC Conquest of Paradise is active, the reform "Settle Down" is enacted.
      • Has a direct land border with a neighbor that fullfills the following conditions :
        • Has embraced Feudalism
        • Does not follow the Inti, Mayan or Nahuatl faith

      Allows enacting the tier 4 reform Trading with foreigners.

      Effects :

      • Removes primitive status if the neighbor was not primitive.
      • Embraced institutions in the feudal neighbor are automatically embraced by the reformed natives.
      • Government changes to match the government form of the nation that the natives are modernizing from, i.e., if a native tribe modernizes from a kingdom then it will reform into a kingdom, same for republics and theocracies. If the nation being modernized from has the Siberian Clan Council government type or steppe nodmads, the tribe reforms into a generic non-primitive tribe.
      • All native buildings (including any already built) and the ability to build them are removed, and the standard building interface will replace the native building interface

      Becoming a Horde[edit | edit source]

      Conditions :

      • If the DLC Conquest of Paradise is active, the reform "Settle Down" is not enacted.
      • Has military technology 6

      Allows enacting the tier 5 reform The Great Plains call to us.

      Effects :

      • Removes primitive status, which allows spawning and embracing institutions.
      • Changes technology group and unit type to nomadic
      • All native buildings, ideas and their effects are removed.
      • Enacts the Tier 1 Steppe Nomads government reform

      Reforming via Feudalism[edit | edit source]

      Conditions :

      • Has embraced the feudalism institution

      Allows enacting the tier 5 reforms Dynastic Lineage, Rule by the Public or State of Faith

      Effects :

      • Removes primitive status, which allows spawning and embracing institutions.
      • All native buildings, ideas and their effects are removed.
      • Changes government type according to the chosen reform

      If Conquest of Paradise is enabled, Native Tribes can't manually spawn Institutions. To gain Feudalism it has to either spread naturally from neighboring provinces, or must be conquered.
