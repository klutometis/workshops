      Sukhothai is a three province Theravada nation in Southeast Asia. It is available up until 1530 as a vassal of Ayutthaya. It borders Lan Na to the North, Ayutthaya to the South, Lan Xang to the East, and Pegu to the West. In past patches, Sukhothai would have to fight the giant Ming in order to gain independence from Ayutthaya, which starts out as the Chinese superpower's tributary, famously making Sukhothai a nearly impossible start. However, with Patch 1.31, an event chain was added that triggers very early on to give Sukhothai independence without needing to fight Ayutthaya's overlord. Additionally, if the player does choose to fight for independence, Ming will no longer be called in to their tributary's war.

      Strategy[edit | edit source]

      The below is one of many player suggested strategies for Sukhothai. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

      Gaining independence[edit | edit source]

      Sukhothai, like its fellow vassal Ligor, starts in a highly precarious position: it is vassal of Ayutthaya, one of the strongest nations in the region and which has a much larger military and economy than Sukhothai. Additionally, Ayutthaya has cores on all of Sukhothai's provinces, meaning that as soon as 1 January 1448, Sukhothai's overlord will be able to instantly diplomatically annex it.

      However, all is not lost. At the beginning of the game, Ayutthaya's heir is Trailok Suphannaphum, a dashing young prince who also happens to be the heir to Sukhothai's throne! When King Borommaracha of Ayutthaya inevitably shuffles off his mortal coil, this will trigger the event The Ascension of Trailok for Sukhothai, which grants the player three options:

      1. Gain independence and make Ayutthaya a vassal, while gaining (but not coring) the provinces of Suphanburi and Phetchabun as well as the entire North Tenasserim area.
      2. Gain independence while allowing Ayutthaya to remain independent.
      3. Remain a vassal of Ayutthaya.

      Making Ayutthaya a vassal is a more risky, but potentially more rewarding, option, as it will grant 62 development's worth of provinces without having to fight a war. Additionally, making Ayutthaya a subject will change the reward of the mission "Restore Sukhothai", making it give cores on every Siamese culture province owned by Ayutthaya. However, Ayutthaya will be an extremely disloyal vassal and will be able to immediately start seeking Sukhothai's enemies' support for their independence since the event chain does not establish a truce between your two countries. Thus, if the player chooses to subjugate Ayutthaya, it is recommended to start improving relations with potential future allies from game start in order to make it easier to immediately ally a couple reasonably strong countries upon gaining independence to discourage them from rebelling.

      Although the challenges associated with subjugating Ayutthaya are surmountable enough that the second option is generally not recommended, if King Borommaracha dies within the first year of the game, it may be difficult to have enough time to make preparations to keep Ayutthaya loyal and you might instead wish to allow Ayutthaya to keep their independence upon gaining yours.

      Remaining a vassal will only waste your admin points and AE since you will need to fight for your independence (giving minus 3 stability), so this option is not recommended.

      It is worth noting that while normally one must wait 10 years before integrating a vassal, Ayutthaya will be able to integrate Sukhothai starting 1 January 1448, or a little over 3 years, and there is no guarantee that King Borommaracha will die before this time. However, there are a couple ways to remain independent despite this danger. For one, Ayutthaya will also need to have +190 relations with Sukhothai in order to begin its integration, and it will take them about 57 months (4 years, 9 months) to sufficiently improve relations at the standard pace of +2 per month. The player can extend this timeline by refusing any royal marriage requests from Ayutthaya (since royal marriages give a mutual +25 opinion bonus) and by not improving relations with them while waiting for King Borommaracha to die (since in addition to improving the target country's opinion of you, improving relations with another country will also improve your own opinion of that country by half of the same amount). Additionally, you will need to have less than 50% liberty desire towards Ayutthaya for them to integrate you. This desire can be increased by getting other countries to support your independence (even if you never fight an independence war) as well as by increasing your own army size. Ayutthaya will usually two or three countries between Lan Xang, Dai Viet, Bengal, and Khmer, among which Lan Xang and Bengal will be willing to support Sukhothai independence if the player improves relations with them enough. Dai Viet will also be willing to support Sukhothai's independence if Ming abandons Ayutthaya's tributary status, as it is wont to do if Ayutthaya expands too much.

      It is important for the player to keep an eye on Sukhothai's liberty desire and its opinion of Ayutthaya in order to know in advance before Ayutthaya beings its annexation. If Ayutthaya is on the brink of gaining the conditions needed for annexation, or if the player doesn't want to wait around for King Borommaracha to kick the bucket, it is also possible to declare independence the normal way, although the player should keep in mind that fighting independence will cost -3 stability upon declaring the independence war.

      Fortunately, unlike in older patches, Ming will not be called in to join in the independence war, making gaining independence far less difficult than in past versions of the game. Do not be alarmed by the "would have to fight tributary overlord" malus that pops up when seeking Khmer or Dai Viet's support for independence, as this is a relic of past patches and does not reflect the reality of current game mechanics. It will, however, make it harder to get Ayutthaya's rivals, most notable Dai Viet to support Sukhothai's independence.

      The extreme measures that used to be needed to gain independence due to Ming's involvement can be reviewed by clicking the "View History" tab at the top of this page and reading past versions of this page.

      Post-independence[edit | edit source]

      Once Sukhothai is independent, the next mission in the mission tree will promise a subjugation CB on Lan Na if Sukhothai directly owns several of Ayutthaya's provinces. If the player wishes to use this CB, they may elect to wait until integrate Ayutthaya can be integrated to unlock this mission. However, you may wish to simply conquer Lan Na directly instead - the last mission in this branch of Sukhothai's mission tree does not differentiate between owning Lan Na's provinces directly or indirectly, and so the player will be able to gain its rewards either way.

      It is recommended that Sukhothai attempt to become a tributary of Ming soon after gaining independence, as doing so will allow Sukhothai to fight Ming's tributary states to Sukhothai's east without calling its massive northern neighbour into the war. This can be done by taking a province that borders Ming from one of their tributaries that has been called into one of Sukhothai's wars as a non-cobelligerent so long as Sukhothai has already conquered the needed land to connect this province with the nation. Some prime targets for this maneuver are Lan Xang's Luang Namtha and any of Mong Mao's provinces.

      At this point, the hard part is over, and the game becomes a classic game of waiting and exploiting opportunities until the player is strong enough to face all comers. By getting one or two powerful neighbors as allies, Sukhothai will not have to be under any immediate threat of being directly attacked by anyone, allowing the player to dictate the pace of the game and look towards its other neighbors for potential conquest, though the player will likely find that some have already consumed others or by Bengal. By expanding in this way, Sukhothai can easily surpass Lan Xang and Khmer and become the dominant force in the area. Sukhothai is in a good position to form Siam, which comes with much stronger national ideas and a more expansive mission tree. Forming Siam requires that Sukhothai own 20 Thai-culture provinces, including Ayutthaya, Sukhothai, and Chiang Mai, which are all easy or trivial to own for Sukhothai, as well as having completed two non-estate disasters.

      Beyond Southeast Asia, there are two main avenues of expansion: the Sunni nations of Bengal to the west and Malacca to the south. Their Islamic territories can cause unrest and lower religious unity, so the player must make sure that they have the needed missionary strength to convert them. Bengal especially is a strong nation at the start of the game and often expands towards Sukhothai's region before the player can do the opposite. This means that the territories bordering the player will often follow the player's Theravada faith, which can be annexed in one go and will not be an overly dangerous source of unrest for the nation. For further aid, Sukhothai may choose to ally a neighboring Indian power, such as Orissa, and enlist their aid when fighting Bengal.

      Over to the south, while Malacca can be a useful ally early on, they will eventually become a stumbling block on the road to expansion, since eliminating them opens a path to the Malaya region, which offers many rich territories for conquest and colonization. Between colonization of the Malayas, hegemony in Southeast Asia, and westward expansion into India, Sukhothai has a tremendous number of options for expansion, and with its formidable army, its brand new navy and its prosperous lands, even Ming may start looking over its shoulder, dreading the inevitable advance of the Thai liberators...
