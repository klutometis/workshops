      Onna-Musha in the Edo Period

      Originally serving as female bushi, the Onna-musha were esteemed warriors in the early days of our Empire. They were members of the samurai class in feudal Japan and were trained in the use of weapons to protect their household, family, and honor in times of war. Over time, they distinguished themselves in the battlefield as able commanders, scoring impressive victories and cementing their names in the books of history.

      However, because of the influence of Edo neo-Confucianism, the status of the Onna-musha began diminishing significantly. The function of Onna-musha changed in addition to their husbands. Samurai were no longer concerned with battles and war, they were bureaucrats. Women, specifically daughters of most upper class households, were soon pawns to dreams of success and power. The roaring ideals of fearless devotion and selflessness were gradually replaced by quiet, passive, civil obedience. Despite this, many still wish to serve the nation's military forces as commanders and humbly request to be allowed to lay their lives in service of our country's noble cause. One notable example of the female bushi icons is Ohori Tsuruhime. She has courageously been to battle several times and her claim to divine inspiration coupled with fighting skills has led to her being compared with the likes of Joan of Arc. Her legacy will surely establish her as one of the most recognizable female warriors in Japanese history.

      Way of Chonindo

      The changes in society after the turbulent times of the Sengoku Jidai are quite obvious. The previous constant struggles across all Japan have finally subsided, and now we enjoy a somewhat stable internal peace which allows a more relaxed life for the common people. Their aspiration towards the samurai class and their values of bushido, together with a keen interest in the development of arts and science have resulted in a distinct new mass culture, focused on a great quality of workmanship. If unnecessary implements of war are kept, the collection of annual rent or nengu will be more difficult, and without provocation uprisings can be fomented. - The reason for the Sword Hunt was just, but the actions performed by your soldiers were not. Instead of only gathering the swords and other weapons, the soldiers also took it upon themselves to punish the sword-bearers. The Ikko-ikki and the Ronin rose in defense and now your country faces a revolt.
