Mamluk Succession

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

The death of [Root.Monarch.GetName] has left our country without a ruler. All the important Mamluk households of [Root.Capital.GetCapitalName] are calling upon their influence to have a say in who his successor is going to be. Depending on who wins our new ruler will have the support of different households and different parts of the country.\nOnly one thing is certain, those that would rather have seen their own group's interests prevailing will not greet our new [Root.Monarch.GetTitle] with joy. Some might even spend the rest of their lives trying to limit his influence.

 
Trigger conditions
  • None
 Is triggered only by

the death of foreign slave ruler.

Immediate effects

Hidden effects:

  • "First ruler alternative" culture:
    • Is our culture or accepted culture of an owned province
  • "Second ruler alternative" culture:
    • Is our culture or accepted culture, but not first one of an owned province
  • "Third ruler alternative" culture:
    • Is our culture or accepted culture, but not first or second one of an owned province
  • "Fourth ruler alternative" culture:
    • Is the culture of a province which:
      • If
        • Is Circassian
        • Is not owned by us
        • Owner is not at war with us, our rival or enemy
        • Is not the first, second or third culture
      • Else:
        • Has been discovered
        • Is owned by a Muslim country which is not at war, our rival or enemy
        • Has slaves as trade goods
        • Is not the first, second or third culture

The skills of all candidates are hidden, unless the government reform Integrated Ottoman Officials is enacted

We need the support of the [first_ruler_alternative_province.Culture.GetName] Mamluks.
  • Get a new ruler with "First ruler alternative" culture and is 30/32/35/40/42/45/50 (randomly) years old
Only the [second_ruler_alternative_province.Culture.GetName] Mamluks are of use now.
  • Get a new ruler with "Second ruler alternative" culture and is 30/32/35/40/42/45/50 (randomly) years old
The [third_ruler_alternative_province.Culture.GetName] candidate has our support!
  • Get a new ruler with "Third ruler alternative" culture and is 30/32/35/40/42/45/50 (randomly) years old
The best alternative is one who has no ties to the groups in [Root.Capital.GetCapitalName]...
  • Gain 15 army tradition
  • Get a new ruler with "Fourth ruler alternative" culture and is 30/32/35/40/42/45/50 (randomly) years old

[not_ruling_culture_province.Culture.GetName] Soldiers

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

While Mamluks make up the core of our forces not all of our military might consists of warrior slaves. Local [not_ruling_culture_province.Culture.GetName] soldiers make up much of our infantry and they are also used for guard and garrison duties.
Small chances of advancement and low pay aren't the best motivators however, and it is not clear how far we can trust these men. Certainly their training and equipment are often neglected by the Amir in charge of them.
In [not_ruling_culture_province.GetAreaName] the mistrust between the [not_ruling_culture_province.Culture.GetName] and Mamluk Amirs have led to a mutiny.

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government" type
  • Does not have country modifiers:
    • "Rebels Crushed"
    • "Placated Local Population"
  • Owns any province which:
    • Culture is accepted
    • Culture is ruler's culture
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
175
We must crush this in its cradle!

If the country has the nobility estate,

  • then the estate gains 10 loyalty,
  • else if the country has the rajputs estate,
    • then the estate gains 10 loyalty,
    • else if the country has the marathas estate,
      • then the estate gains 10 loyalty.
  • Gain country modifier "Rebels Crushed" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • +1 global unrest
  • Nationalist rebels (size 2) rise in revolt in trigger province
Let us see what we can do to improve their situation.

If the country has the burghers estate,

  • then the estate gains 10 loyalty,
  • else if the country has the vaishyas estate,
    • then the estate gains 10 loyalty,
    • else if the country has the jains estate,
      • then the estate gains 10 loyalty.
  • Lose ducats equal to 0.15 years of income
  • Gain country modifier "Placated Local Population" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • +5% global manpower modifier

The Tarh and the Royal Monopolies

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

Our predecessors have wisely used a system of royal monopolies to control the revenue from cash crops and fine goods. With demand falling however we are losing money on these investments. Our overseer in [monopoly_province.GetName] is reporting that they have been unable to sell their stock for two years now and worry that this is going to cost the state money.
He suggests that we force our subjects to buy these goods at a set price instead so that we can regain our investment. A process traditionally known as Tarh.

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government", "Iqta" or "Indian Sultanate" type
  • Owns any province which:
    • Is part of a state
    • Produces either sugar, cotton, paper, silk, cloth, gems or coffee
    • Does not have province modifiers "Unprofitable Monopolies" or "Tarh"
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
175
What an excellent idea!

If the country has the burghers estate,

  • then the estate loses 10 loyalty,
  • else if the country has the vaishyas estate,
    • then the estate loses 10 loyalty,
    • else if the country has the jains estate,
      • then the estate loses 10 loyalty.
  • Gain ducats equal to 0.15 years of income
  • A random province following the trigger conditions:
    • Gain province modifier "Tarh" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
      • -25% trade goods size
We will just have to endure the cost.

If the country has the burghers estate,

  • then the estate gains 10 loyalty,
  • else if the country has the vaishyas estate,
    • then the estate gains 10 loyalty,
    • else if the country has the jains estate,
      • then the estate gains 10 loyalty.
  • Lose ducats equal to 0.15 years of income
  • A random province following the trigger conditions:
    • Gain province modifier "Unprofitable Monopolies" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
      • -50% trade value modifier

Factionalism

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

The [Root.Monarch.GetTitle] is far from the only owner of Mamluks in our [Root.GovernmentName]. The households of influential manumitted Mamluks often contain great, if not greater, numbers of newly imported slaves from their home regions. These influential Amirs are often those with the greatest influence during a succession but even when the throne is occupied the influence of the Mamluk households are great on [Root.GetAdjective] politics. It is up to [Root.Monarch.GetName] to ensure that this factionalism does not stifle the politics of [Root.Capital.GetCapitalName].

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government" type
  • Does not have country modifiers:
    • "Factionalism"
    • "Ruler Supporting Faction"
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
235
We must attempt to play them off against each other.
  • Gain country modifier "Factionalism" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • +15% stability cost modifier
    • +0.5 yearly legitimacy
Let us pick sides and support the stronger factions.
  • Gain country modifier "Ruler Supporting Faction" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • +15% global manpower modifier
    • -0.5 yearly legitimacy

The City of Cairo

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

Much older than our own [Root.GovernmentName], Cairo has been embellished by rulers and conquerors for hundreds if not a thousand years. Bringing order in this maze of buildings, from the tombs of the city of the dead and the slums of Fustat, to the bustling markets of Khan el-Khalili, or the great Citadel of Cairo itself, has proven a monumental task for many of our predecessors. Nonetheless, as the master of the city, [Root.Monarch.GetName], is expected to leave [Root.Monarch.GetHerHis] mark upon Cairo, both to embellish and develop it, and to combat disease and keep the order.

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government" type
  • Our capital
    • Is Cairo (361)
    • Does not have one of the below modifiers
  • Does not have ruler flag "No pork for Cairo"
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
175
Let us renovate the souks and the Khan el-Khalili.
  • Our capital gains province modifier "Expanding the Markets of Cairo" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • -50% local tax modifier
    • +25% trade goods size
    • +10% trade value modifier
We must renovate the growing slums and the suburbs.
  • Our capital gains province modifier "Renovation of the Slums of Cairo" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • -15% local tax modifier
    • -15% development cost
    • +10% local build cost
The Mosques and the al-Azhar require our attention.
  • Our capital gains province modifier "Patronization of al-Azhar" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • -50% local tax modifier
    • -1% technology cost
    • +0.2 monthly piety
    • -50% local production efficiency
Let us focus on the citadel itself, and the defenses of the city.
  • Our capital gains province modifier "Citadel of Cairo" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • +25% local defensiveness
    • -15% local build cost
Coming generations shall envy the monuments we erect.
  • Our capital gains province modifier "Monuments of Cairo" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
    • +0.5 yearly legitimacy
    • +15% local development cost
There is not much we can do for the citizens of Cairo right now.
  • Lose 5 Legitimacy
  • Set ruler flag "No pork for Cairo"

A New Amir al-Hajj

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

The pilgrimage to the holy cities of Mecca and Medina is one that every Muslim should undertake at least once in their lifetime. As the [Root.Monarch.GetTitle] it is our responsibility to ensure that they can safely reach that destination. This is a position that in many ways requires both cunning and martial ability, as the way to proceed can range from defeating raiders to negotiating passage from local rulers, to outright threats or bribery. The Amir al-Hajj will have the overall responsibility for the Hajj caravans and will have great freedom in how to achieve a steady and peaceful passage for all [Root.GetAdjective] pilgrims.

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Is Muslim
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government", "Iqta" or "Tribal Federation" type
  • Does not have one of the below modifiers
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
250

 Enabled if: Primary culture is Bedouin

Let us choose a Bedouin, the best of our own.
  • Gain 5 Legitimacy
  • Gain country modifier "Bedouin Amir al-Hajj" for 30 years, giving the following effects:
    • +10% global manpower modifier
    • -0.5 yearly prestige

 Enabled if: Primary culture is not Bedouin

Let us choose a Bedouin, native to the deserts of Arabia.
  • Lose 10 Prestige
  • Gain country modifier "Bedouin Amir al-Hajj" for 30 years, giving the following effects:
    • +10% global manpower modifier
    • -0.5 yearly prestige

 Enabled if: Primary culture is not Bedouin

A skilled warrior will be up for the task.
  • Lose 35 Military power
  • Gain country modifier "Warrior Amir al-Hajj" for 30 years, giving the following effects:
    • +0.5 yearly prestige

 Always enabled:

An able diplomat will handle this best.
  • Lose 35 Diplomatic power
  • Gain country modifier "Diplomatic Amir al-Hajj" for 30 years, giving the following effects:
    • +5% trade efficiency

 Enabled if: Has enacted "Mamluk Government" type

Only a loyal Mamluk can perform this task.
  • Gain 5 Legitimacy
  • Gain country modifier "Mamluk Amir al-Hajj" for 30 years, giving the following effects:
    • +10% global manpower modifier

Awlad al-nas

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

Having been educated to be loyal warriors since early age Mamluk slaves often have the complete trust of their owner or employer. Their sons however, the Awlad al-nas, having grown up under far more convenient and forgiving circumstances, are generally considered far less able and loyal. Unable to serve as Royal Mamluks the Awlad al-nas instead constitute a social elite within regular military units and in the lower administration.
These noble sons themselves are however continually trying to prove that they are ready for bigger responsibilities. With the open position of command for the defenses of the [defence_province.GetAreaName] state, a number of them are hoping that we will entrust them with this task.

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government" type
  • Owns any province which:
    • Is part of a state
    • Neighbors a province we do not own
    • Does not have province modifiers
      • "Commander from the Awlad al-nas"
      • "Mamluk Commander"
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
185
Let us appoint one of the sons.
  • Lose 5 Legitimacy
  • All owned provinces in province's area described in triger conditions:
    • Gain province modifier "Commander from the Awlad al-nas" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
      • +15% local defensiveness
      • +25% local manpower modifier
We can only trust our own, the commander must be a Mamluk.
  • Gain 5 Legitimacy
  • All owned provinces in province's area described in triger conditions:
    • Gain province modifier "Mamluk Commander" for 10 years, giving the following effects:
      • -4 local unrest
      • +15% local defensiveness

Furusiyya

Please help with verifying or updating this infobox. It was last verified for version 1.30.

The Furusiyya are a set of equestrian exercises that teach young warriors the craft of cavalry warfare. With detailed rules, training manuals and very popular public competitions and champions, the Furusiyya has in many ways been perfected by Mamluk warrior aristocracy. While some chroniclers would claim that the champions of today could never compete with the warriors of the past, it is still both customary, and very popular, for [Root.Monarch.GetTitle]s to arrange big public Furusiyya competitions, and the champions remain the heroes of many popular tales.

Available only with the Cradle of Civilization DLC enabled.
 
Trigger conditions
  • Has enacted "Mamluk Government" type
  • Is not at war
  • Owns at least 10 provinces
  • Does not have country modifier "Furusiyya"
 Is triggered only by

the bi-yearly pulse I.

Base weight
235
This is good for the people, but not something for a [Root.Monarch.GetTitle] to spend money on.
  • Gain 5 Legitimacy
  • Gain 10 army tradition
Let us expand and build new hippodromes to patronize the flowers of chivalry.
  • Lose ducats equal to 0.25 years of income
  • Lose manpower equal to 1 year of manpower
  • Gain 5 Legitimacy
  • Gain 25 army tradition
  • Gain country modifier "Furusiyya" for 5 years, giving the following effects:
    • +10% cavalry power
    • +10% cavalry cost
