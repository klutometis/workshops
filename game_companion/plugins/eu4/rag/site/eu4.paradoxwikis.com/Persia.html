      Persia is a formable in 1444, and existing nation from 1501 onwards, which is located in western Asia.

      Historically, Persia is one of the oldest civilisations in the world, with its origins traceable back to the fourth millenium BC, with the Iranian Plateau first being unified in the sixth century BC. This was followed by a succession of Persian empires from 550 BC to the Muslim conquest of Persia in 632-651 AD. The state of Persia depicted in-game was formed in the early 16th century by the Safavid dynasty and their Qizilbash (half tribe; half "military quasi-order") supporters, which exist in-game as Ardabil. After the initial military success, Persia went on the defensive. During the reign of Abbas the Great the process of modernization of the state began, though this stopped after the fall of the subsequent Afsharid dynasty. Subsequent governments of Persia (those being the Zand and Qajar dynasties) lost possessions in the east, west, and north, but Persia would still persist as a regional power into the modern era. In 1925, the Pahlavi dynasty took power in Persia, and in 1935, a decree was issued where foreign diplomats were asked to refer to Persia by its endonym, Iran, creating the predecessor to modern-day Iran.

      Starting in 1444 the following countries can form Persia: Afghanistan, Ajam, Ardabil, Ardalan, Baluchistan, Biapas, Fars, Gilan, Khorasan, Luristan, Mazandaran, Qara Qoyunlu, Sistan, and Shirvan. Due to special events, mission trees and other conditions the following countries outside of the Iranian culture group in 1444 can also form Persia: Aq Qoyunlu and the Timurids.

      Missions[edit | edit source]

      Main article: Persian missions

      Persia has an extensive mission tree involving realizing historical claims across the Middle East, Balkans, and India, building extensive mountain forts, choosing between Shia, Sunni, and Zoroastrian religious paths, as well as extensive internal economic and military development.

      Without King of Kings, the Persian mission tree is much smaller. focusing on uniting the Persia region and then conquering the neighboring regions of Azerbaijan, Khorasan and Afghanistan, similar to how Persia did historically.

      Events[edit | edit source]

      Main article: Persian events

      Persian historical events focus on the various Ottoman-Safavid wars as well as the political reforms & turmoil faced by the empire in its later stages.

      Strategy[edit | edit source]

      The below is one of many player suggested strategies for Persia. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.

      Depending on the country used and the position you are in, your path as Persia will differ.

      The Persian mission tree is fairly powerful, especially regarding permanent claims as well as gaining permanent modifiers applicable for the remainder of the game. It is therefore advised to carefully map out one's progress given these powerful bonuses. Moreover, be sure to analyze the "national decisions" for those that provide permanent modifiers valid until the end of the game. Some require certain provinces in order to take the decision, for example, "Rekindling the Royal Fires" which provides a substantial +20% increase in manpower in true faith provinces.

      To form Persia it is suggested to start as Ajam, which is generally the easiest path. Also, Persia is the tag most suitable to convert to Zoroastrianism and gain from its unique bonuses, especially coupled with the Deus Vult from Religious ideas as no one in the world will share its religion, allowing you to invade anyone you desire.

      Make sure the Timurids are defeated as early as possible and you take Afghanistan as a vassal and give them all of their cores back, then integrate them later. If the Afghans are not made into a vassal and are left alone, they will often grow into a decently-sized nation which, coupled with the rough terrain and their national ideas, can make them a nightmare to invade and siege down. After you have taken care of the minors in the area, you can expand in many directions. Integrating Afghanistan and expanding into Baluchistan have the benefit of having Iranian Cultures. You can expand into the Caucasus and either vassalize the Christians, or annex them to strengthen the Dhimmi estate. Make sure you expand north, through Khorasan, Moghulistan and Uzbek before Russia gets there.

      Expanding West though, is most fun. You can play the Ottomans and Mamluks against each other in their battle for Syria, while you gradually crush Aq Qoyunlu and Qara Qoyunlu to conquer Mesopotamia/Iraq. Many of these provinces have Kurdish Culture, but for the Arab provinces, you can convert them to any of the Iranian cultures. If the Mamluks end up winning over the Ottomans, a good late-game ally is Spain, since they often rival the Mamluks.

      Make sure you convert all provinces to Shia, besides maybe Yazd. The Zoroastrians usually don't cause problems. When expanding west into Mesopotamia, don't forget to build good forts. Unlike the mountainous plateau of Persia Proper, Mesopotamia's flat terrain makes it really easy for enemies to roll over.

      An alternative is to build Persia into the premiere Trade power in Asia. Once formed, Persia has the optimal position to monopolize high-value silk Trade Nodes as well as other key nodes in Arabia and Africa. Conquer the East African Coast, Arabia, and Central Asia to transfer a significant trade income into the Persian Node. Using trade companies and the Trade Idea group Persia can build a very healthy early-game trade income. If the player is playing tall this base can be built into a massive 400/month income without too much additional conquest. If the player wishes to play a more conquest-oriented game Persia is optimally suited for this as well. After formation, Persia will have some tough wars to fight and substantial development to stay modern. If the player waits till around 1530 to launch major offensive operations they will be on par with those in the West and 2 tech levels ahead of those in the East. Don't attack in the West and try to build a long-term alliance with either the Mamluks or Ottomans. Slowly reduce the Timurids or whoever controls the Eastern Iranian area. After that roll into India. India should be controlled by 5 or 6 nations that have big, yet outdated armies. Defeat them and take a few provinces but mainly take money. Rinse and repeat until you control the whole sub-continent. At this point, your trade income will be extremely high and you can do whatever you want going forward.
