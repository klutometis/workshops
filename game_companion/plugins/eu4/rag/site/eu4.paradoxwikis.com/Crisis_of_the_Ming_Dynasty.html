      Crisis of the Ming Dynasty
      The Ming dynasty is crumbling due to internal strife. They are at risk of losing the Mandate.

      Crisis of the Ming Dynasty[1] is a disaster in Ming after the ‘Age of Discovery’.

      Strategy to navigate the crisis[edit | edit source]

      Preparation before triggering the disaster[edit | edit source]

      If you have Mandate of Heaven, then as long as you are emperor of China, the disaster will happen at some point after the Age of Discovery after you pass a reform, since doing so causes your mandate to drop to no more than 20. Under ideal conditions it will take 50 months to trigger; to consistently avoid this you need a monthly mandate growth of at least +0.6, which is well beyond what you're likely to be able to achieve in the early 16th century.

      Your strategy then should be to put yourself in the best possible position to counter all the problems you will be facing, and trigger it at a time of your choosing by passing a reform. Consider the immediate effects of the disaster starting (losing stability and mandate and gaining corruption), and the ongoing issues (unrest, economic and tech cost penalties, military penalties, and devastation).

      Stability is easy enough to deal with: simply bank enough administrative power to boost up to +1 after the disaster starts (with your Inward Perfection national idea this shouldn't be more than 180). You only need to boost to +3 if you don't have Mandate of Heaven, but doing so does help with other penalties such as tax and corruption.

      Similarly corruption is not a major problem: bank enough money to root it out and it will drop quickly enough. You should also concentrate on arranging for other modifiers to corruption, such as being ahead of time in tech, not being overextended, and especially having high harmony. Don't start harmonizing a religion near the end of the Age of Discovery.

      For the economic penalties, you can counter them by building your economy to have a large surplus. A balance while your armies are at full maintenance equal to about a third of your income should be enough, but it's advisable to also gather a war chest in case balance does go negative. Save up lots of cash, on the order of thousands of ducats.

      Devastation: Make sure it is 0 everywhere. The devastation increase simply negates the reduction from having control of a province you own, so devastation will not normally actually increase, but it won't decrease either, unless it's in zone of control of an active fort.

      To minimize the time spent in the disaster, you want to raise mandate growth as high as possible. Arranging this starts from day 1 and involves getting as many tributaries as possible, having prosperity in all your states, and potentially taking full Court ideas and upgrading the Forbidden City to Magnificent level.

      Triggering the crisis[edit | edit source]

      The loss of mandate is the most complex of the immediate problems to deal with. Remember that the disaster won't start if you have 50 or more mandate. Ideally you will want to time passing a reform at the end of the Age of Discovery so that you will end up with just under 50 mandate right before the disaster starts, so you start the disaster with the maximum possible of just under 15 mandate. Passing a reform leaves you with 20 mandate if you start at 100, so you want to gain a total of just under 30 mandate between passing the reform and starting the disaster. When you see the "Age of Discovery is ending" alert (typically the first decade or two of the 1500s), start planning when to trigger the disaster.

      1. Calculate how long it will take for the disaster to start, assuming mandate remains between 30 and 50. If you take 5 loans (ideally from the Indebted to the Shangbang privilege), have no corruption and are not bankrupt, this should be (100 / 3) rounded up = 34 months.
      2. Multiply this number by your monthly mandate gain. This is how much mandate you will gain between starting the Age of Reformation and getting the disaster.
      3. Subtract this number from 30. This is how much mandate you want to gain before starting the Age of Reformation (assuming you start at 20).
      4. Divide this number by your monthly mandate gain.

      The resulting number is how many months before the end of the Age of Discovery you should pass a reform. You should generally err on the side of taking the reform later rather than sooner, otherwise you risk having the disaster fail to start due to too much mandate. Be careful with events that change your mandate.

      You can also calculate how much mandate you can spend or waste before you need to pass a reform:

      • Multiply the number of months left in the Age of Discovery by your monthly mandate growth.
      • Subtract the number calculated in step 3 above.
      • Add your current mandate, then subtract 100.

      Once the Age of Reformation starts, the disaster should immediately start ticking at +2 per month. At this point, issue the "Indebted to the Shangbang" privilege to raise this to +3. You should also reevaluate whether the disaster will trigger before mandate reaches 50. If it's close, consider taking an additional 5 loans, which will double the ticking speed to +6 (you don't necessarily have to do this immediately). Be careful not to spend the money you get from these loans, because you'll want to pay them off as soon as possible.

      During the disaster[edit | edit source]

      First, pay off your loans and raise stability back to +1. There's no reason to be sitting at negative stability and paying excess interest. If you have enough administrative power, it may be worth raising it to +3 to counteract several penalties, especially corruption and tax, and to increase your mandate growth.

      If you find yourself with a negative balance, you may be able to make up the difference by firing advisors and hiring lower level ones. It helps if you start with high meritocracy to reduce the cost of your advisors. Consider also raising money by selling titles. Your biggest expense is probably your army, so it may be worth disbanding some of them, but remember that will make your enemies see you as weak, as well as make it harder to fight rebels. On no account should you mothball forts. Take loans if you need to rather than risk rebels spawning on a mothballed fort. You need fewer than 5 loans to end the disaster, but better to delay it a bit than run out of manpower.

      Raise the Root Out Corruption slider. Use your banked wealth to fund it – this is part of what it's for, so don't be afraid to go into deficit.

      You will only be able to deal with the horrendous unrest by fighting rebel armies. With +15 national unrest (actually starting at +18.5 thanks to low mandate) there's little you can do to avoid this. Rebel armies have the same combat modifiers as yours, except they are not affected by morale modifiers, so you will typically be at a disadvantage in quality. High prestige and army tradition will help (as would power projection, but with your lack of rivals you have no way to increase it). Always attack with superior numbers and a good general, and take advantage of favourable terrain where possible. More cavalry and artillery also help. If you run low on manpower, consider slackening recruitment or hiring mercenaries (but be careful with loans).

      To end the disaster, you need to raise mandate to 75, so you need to gain a bit more than 60. This would normally take about 15 years (obviously dependent on how good your mandate growth is), but you can reduce that significantly whenever you get an event that allows you to increase mandate. This is another reason to start with high meritocracy: these events usually trade it off against mandate.
